## 3 · Starting a Life Sciences Tech Company

[SIT · bench or café table, outside, natural light · head-on or 3/4]
[BROLL cutaways: laptop, early Datavial UI · b-roll of LA / lab-adjacent texture]

VO (the why):
I spent years building software inside biotech operations.
And over the last couple of years I watched the quality
of what we shipped start to slip.

VO:
AI changed what teams were expected to deliver.
Work moved offshore. The bar dropped.
And I could see exactly what needed fixing.

VO (the wall):
The problem was, I couldn't fix it from where I sat.
Internal politics. Budgets. The way these organizations
are structured. The good ideas died in the structure.

VO (the decision):
So I left. At 34. To build it from the outside,
where the structure isn't in the way.

VO:
I'm not going to pretend I have it all figured out.
I don't. But I know what's broken, and I know how to build.

## 4 · Sector Analysis: Qualifications Management

[BROLL: early UI · mermaid schema screen-capture · clean, brief]
[Optional simple on-screen labels: LMS · Workday · Scheduling]

VO:
Let me show you the specific problem I'm starting with.

VO:
In a lab, before anyone can run a method, a bioassay,
they have to be qualified to run it. Trained. Current. Signed off.

VO:
And right now, the tools that are supposed to answer
"who is qualified to run this, today" don't actually answer it.

VO (the three):
Your LMS knows who finished a training course.
It doesn't know if you can be assigned to a protocol today.
Workday knows who you are and what you're paid.
It doesn't know if your method qualification expired last week.
Scheduling knows who's available.
It'll happily schedule someone for work they're not qualified to do.

VO (the scar · healed, no employer named):
At one company, multiple sites were each keeping spreadsheets
to track who was qualified to run which method.
Eventually they paid for custom software to fix it,
and spent a fortune on something that didn't need to be that expensive.

VO:
The integration was possible. It was just done the expensive way.
That's the gap. Not that this can't be solved.
That it's being solved badly, at great cost, over and over.
