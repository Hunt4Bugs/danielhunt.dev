# Short-form anatomy

## Reusable synthesis

For a short-form video, pair a visual Scroll Stopper with a verbal Hook at the opening, carry the audience promise through Development, and fulfill it in the Payoff.

| Beat | Job | Required decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scroll Stopper | Make the first visual frame legible and worth pausing for. | Visual hook type, first frame, visual intent. |
| Verbal Hook | Establish an audience promise or open loop. | Verbal hook type, spoken/on-screen line, promise. |
| Development | Earn the payoff through proof, observation, story, or example. | Ordered beats and supporting evidence. |
| Payoff | Fulfill the promise without prematurely signaling the end. | Closing line/image and intended audience feeling. |

## Source-specific guidance

For a typical 60-second short, the Source suggests a 0–3 second Scroll Stopper, a 0–5 second verbal Hook, and a final 5–10 second Payoff. These are template guidance, not universal production rules.

## Brand constraints

Use real footage, real observations, and supported claims. A hook may create curiosity, but it may not invent stakes, outcomes, customers, or facts. Apply the Brand's documentary restraint and observation-over-preaching principles when selecting hook types.
