# Content production

This document owns reusable production practice, craft development, and preparation checklists. The [Content operating plan](operating-plan.md) owns cadence and launch gates. Asset semantics live in [Assets](concepts.md#asset).

## Current technical production practice

LinkedIn teaching is the active production priority. Begin with an engineering problem supported by attributable experience or a clearly identified investigation.

1. State the problem and its intended reader.
2. Prepare the smallest useful code example, data model, diagram, or worked scenario. Use synthetic records when illustrating a pattern and label them visibly.
3. Explain the decision and at least one relevant alternative or tradeoff.
4. Verify the example's claimed behavior. For code, exercise the failure or repeated-execution case being taught; for a conceptual diagram, check its assumptions and consistency with the explanation.
5. State limits: what was observed, what was tested, what remains a hypothesis, and where the approach may not apply.
6. Edit for clear platform-native writing and useful visual labels. Match the chosen Format to the explanation rather than turning every lesson into a production-heavy asset.

A technical lesson does not require cinematic footage or a personal-life story. Thursday's shorter observation may reuse Tuesday's preparation while contributing a distinct decision or tradeoff. Reusable materials follow the existing Source, Knowledge, and Asset workflows; an example is not automatically a customer case study.

## Review checklist

- [ ] The lesson has an identifiable engineering decision or useful observation.
- [ ] Professional recollection, synthetic demonstration, proposed design, and measured outcome are distinguished.
- [ ] Any behavior or improvement claimed has matching evidence, with the verification method stated.
- [ ] The example is readable at the intended viewing size and all terminology needed by a cross-industry reader is explained.
- [ ] No private employer implementation, unsupported metric, or fabricated incident is presented as public evidence.
- [ ] Time spent is available for the monthly effort review; optional reuse creates no new quota.

## Optional cinematic production grammar

The following practices apply only when choosing to create cinematic material. They are not active daily or weekly requirements and do not gate LinkedIn publication.

Use one documentary production grammar across Build and Offline footage. YouTube is horizontal-first. Capture opportunistic vertical material without restaging candid moments solely for another aspect ratio. Talking-head material supports the story.

Use DaVinci Resolve for editing, color, and Fairlight audio. Preserve documentary restraint, real footage, memory-not-photoshoot, and observation-over-preaching. Avoid the teal-green grade.

## Optional batching and file practice

- Shoot enough raw material for the intended edit; travel may provide footage for several episodes without creating an episode quota.
- Use one documented folder structure, naming convention, proxy workflow, and backup process per shoot.
- Plan cross-format reuse where useful without forcing each moment into every platform.
- Represent required Script or Publication visuals/audio through the ontology-defined Production Dependency State.

## Future craft preparation

When a future video project is chosen, practice the necessary import, cut, color, audio, and export workflow. Study existing internal production references, rehearse voiceover where useful, and verify equipment and backup arrangements. The former daily Resolve/on-camera practice and four-Reel requirements are no longer active commitments.

A future production manual should cover organization, ingestion, proxies, backup, edit, review, and export. Execute it end to end twice before treating it as a proven workflow, in accordance with the retained [YouTube readiness gates](operating-plan.md#future-youtube-readiness).

Production learning creates reusable Knowledge only when captured through the normal Source and Knowledge workflows. Keep production-process meta-content backstage unless it supplies an evidence-backed lesson useful to the audience.
