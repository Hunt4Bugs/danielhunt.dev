## Positioning

> **I help businesses replace spreadsheets and manual work with custom software, automation, and AI — while building everything in public.**

One sentence, two jobs. The first half is the commercial offer: who it helps, what it replaces, what it replaces it with. The second half is the distribution engine: the work is documented openly, and the documentation is what builds trust before any sales conversation happens.

This sentence is the brand's introduction everywhere it introduces itself: the site hero, the LinkedIn headline, platform bios, and how Daniel describes the work to a stranger.

## Thesis

**Build in public. Live offline.**

Great systems create freedom. The brand shares how software gets designed, workflows get automated, and products get built — in public, so others can learn from the process. The goal of every system is the same: less time managing work, more time living life.

The two halves reinforce each other:

- **Build in public** is the proof of competence. Real builds, real decisions, real lessons — shown, not claimed.
- **Live offline** is the proof of outcome. The systems work, so life happens away from the screen. Travel, movement, presence — this is what the freedom is *for*.

The contradiction is still the brand: a software person whose end goal is less screen time, not more.

## Core services

What the brand sells. Three services, one arc: take work that lives in spreadsheets, inboxes, and manual processes, and turn it into systems.

**1. Workflow automation.** Eliminate repetitive work through automation and AI. Replacing manual workflows, AI-powered document processing, approval workflows, internal AI assistants, business process automation, spreadsheet automation.

**2. Custom internal software.** Software tailored to a company's operations instead of forcing them into generic SaaS tools. Internal dashboards, admin portals, operations software, scheduling systems, inventory management, customer portals, team management systems.

**3. Data & reporting.** Transform disconnected business data into actionable insights. Executive dashboards, KPI reporting, business intelligence, ETL pipelines, analytics platforms, reporting automation.

## Content pillars

Five pillars. Every piece of content belongs to at least one.

1. **Workflow automation** — replacing spreadsheets, business automation, AI workflows, process optimization, internal tools.
2. **Custom software** — internal applications, business systems, operations software, dashboards, architecture decisions, product design.
3. **AI for business** — AI coding, Cursor, Claude Code, MCPs, documentation, AI architecture, practical AI workflows.
4. **Build in public** — products being built, lessons learned, wins and failures, experiments, SaaS ideas, technical decisions.
5. **Systems thinking** — productivity, documentation, architecture, simplicity, the automation mindset, designing scalable systems.

Formats, platform roles, and the competitor-audit program live in [`OPERATING.md`](OPERATING.md).

## Points of view

The recurring opinions the brand argues for. Content keeps returning to these; they are the editorial compass and the source of the opinion-piece format.

- **AI should simplify, not complicate.** You don't need 20 AI agents.
- **Most automations are over-engineered.** The best system is the simplest one that removes the work.
- **Documentation beats prompting.** Well-documented systems make both humans and AI effective.
- **Generic SaaS is not the default answer.** Software should fit the operation, not the other way around.

## Voice

Practical, specific, plain. The brand teaches rather than performs: real processes, real numbers, real artifacts on screen. Lessons are shared retrospectively — what was learned, not live crises — and failures get the same airtime as wins. No hype vocabulary; if a tool or technique is recommended, it is one actually in use.

## Audience

- **Clients — the commercial audience.** Owners, operators, and ops leaders at businesses that run on spreadsheets, email chains, and manual processes. Reached primarily through LinkedIn, referrals, and educational content.
- **Builders — the content audience.** Developers, technical founders, indie hackers, and consultants who follow the build-in-public journey and the practical-AI workflow content.

## What the brand is

- A working practitioner showing the work: real client problems, real builds, real lessons
- Educational first: teach the process, not just the outcome
- Simple over clever: automation that removes work, not automation theater
- Honest in public: wins and failures both

## What the brand is not

- Not a hype brand: no AI maximalism, no tool-of-the-week churn
- Not a guru or course-funnel brand
- Not hustle culture: the systems exist to buy time back, not to glorify grind
- Not metrics theater: build in public means decisions and lessons, not vanity dashboards

## Long-term vision

Every client engagement should:

1. Solve a real business problem.
2. Generate content and lessons to share publicly.
3. Reveal repeatable patterns.
4. Become reusable frameworks or software.
5. Eventually evolve into products.

**The flywheel: Content → Trust → Clients → Experience → Products → More Content.**

Services fund the practice, content compounds trust, patterns become products.

## Brand role (the site)

The site is a credibility surface and a contact path — not a content publication. Content lives on LinkedIn, YouTube, X, Instagram, and TikTok. The site states the positioning, shows the services, and routes to email or the contact form.

## Aesthetic tie-break

When choices conflict: **human / lived / curious wins over polished / aspirational. Always.**
