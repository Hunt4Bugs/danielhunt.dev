## Audience tiers

### (C) Clients — primary commercial audience

Owners, operators, and ops leaders at businesses that run on spreadsheets, email chains, and manual processes. They do not care about the stack; they care about time back, fewer errors, and visibility into their own operations.

Where they are reached: LinkedIn first, then referrals, then educational content that gets shared or found.

What content does for them: demonstrates competence before the first call (before/after transformations, workflow teardowns, quick demos), builds trust through visible work, and gives them vocabulary for what they actually need — often they know the pain but not that "replace the spreadsheet with software" is a purchasable thing.

### (B) Builders — primary content audience

Developers, technical founders, indie hackers, and automation consultants following the build-in-public journey. They come for practical AI workflows (Cursor, Claude Code, MCPs), architecture decisions, and honest lessons — wins and failures both.

Where they are reached: X and YouTube first; build-in-public updates travel everywhere.

What content does for them: teaches things they can use the same day, models a working solo practice, and creates the ambient network that produces referrals, collaborations, and distribution.

### (P) Peers — network, not audience

Other consultants, agency operators, and technical creators working the same space. Spoken-with, not spoken-to. Source of referrals, collaborations, and competitive learning — this is the population the competitor-audit program in [`OPERATING.md`](OPERATING.md) studies.

## Client pains v0

In the client's voice. Validate against at least five real client conversations.

1. "Our whole operation runs on spreadsheets and one person who understands them."
2. "My team spends hours every week copying data between systems."
3. "I have no visibility — I find out about problems weeks late."
4. "We bought SaaS tools that almost fit, and now we work around them every day."
5. "Approvals live in email threads and things fall through the cracks."
6. "I know AI could help us, but I can't tell what's real and what's hype."

## Builder pains v0

1. "AI coding hype is everywhere; I want to watch someone actually ship with it."
2. "I want practical workflows — Cursor, Claude Code, MCPs — not tool reviews."
3. "I'm building alone and want to see someone else's real decisions, including the failures."

## Audience-to-platform map

| Audience | Primary platforms | Content that serves them |
|---|---|---|
| Clients (C) | LinkedIn, YouTube | Educational, before/after, workflow teardowns, "what I'd do", opinion pieces, storytelling |
| Builders (B) | X, YouTube, TikTok | Build-in-public updates, behind the scenes, AI-for-business workflows, mistakes & lessons |
| Both | Instagram, TikTok | Quick demos, storytelling, travel and lifestyle — the live-offline proof that the systems work |

## What each audience should leave with

- **Clients:** "This person could remove real work from my business, and I can watch how they think before I ever talk to them."
- **Builders:** "I learned something I can use today, from someone actually doing the work."

Neither audience should leave with: pressure, FOMO, tool-churn anxiety, or the sense of being sold a course.
