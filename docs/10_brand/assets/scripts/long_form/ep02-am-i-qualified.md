## Series note

Journey-driven episode, mostly personal register. The "am I qualified?" question is the spine; Muay Thai is the anchor that makes it physical (a beginner doing it anyway). The product chapter is substantial, not a thin thread: building the qualifications module, where the software's question mirrors the personal one. The dual meaning is used here because it is genuinely sharp · do not force this structure on later episodes.

Girlfriend: present but peripheral. In-frame, rarely named, never the subject, not a content obligation. Brief her before the trip so filming stays relaxed.

Visual contrast that does narrative work: Episode 1 is Ventura (local, grounded). Episode 2 is Thailand (departure, distance). Physically leaving to ask "am I qualified" mirrors the internal question.

Segment order:
1. Intro
2. Arrival
3. Muay Thai (the anchor)
4. The Qualifications Build (product)
5. Yoga + human texture
6. Food
7. The payoff

## 4 · The Qualifications Build (product)

[CAFÉ · laptop open · Datavial qualifications module on screen]
[BROLL: early UI · mermaid schema · a real design decision]
[MUSIC: quieter, focused]

VO:
Here's the strange part. The thing I'm building
is software that answers exactly that question.

VO:
In a lab, before you run a method, you have to be
qualified to run it. Trained. Current. Signed off.
I'm building the system that tracks that.

VO (the modelling problem):
And it's a genuinely hard thing to model.
What counts as qualified. When it expires.
Who's allowed to sign off. How sites disagree.

VO (the collision):
So I'm sitting in a café in Thailand, writing software
that decides who's qualified to do their work,
while I have no idea if I'm qualified to do mine.

VO:
The software gets a clean answer. I don't.
Maybe that's the difference between systems and people.

## Notes

- Target length: 12–15 min · ~13 min designed
- Register is mostly personal. The product chapter is substantial (~2.5–3 min) so the build-in-public promise stays honest, but the emotional weight is the impostor question
- The dual-meaning structure is load-bearing here because the parallel is genuinely sharp. Do not reuse this mirror device every episode · it becomes a formula the audience clocks
- GF: present but peripheral. In-frame, rarely named, never the subject. Confirm her comfort before the trip
- California-food departure beat (Intro) pairs with a return-to-LA-food beat in whatever episode covers coming home. The palate marks departure and homecoming. Category, not a fixed prop · keeps it from going gimmicky
- Capture discipline is the real risk on this trip. See checklist. The failure mode is a great trip and not enough usable footage. Shoot constantly · naturalism comes from volume, not from leaving the camera in the bag
- Thailand is a batch shoot for 3–4 episodes. Capture far more than Episode 2 needs · bank the rest (shopping, Sri Racha, nice dinners, more beach) for later episodes and short-form
- Protect Thailand by not making it your first shoot · run the Ventura shoot first as the production shakedown
